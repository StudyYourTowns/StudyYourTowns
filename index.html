Could Three Towns Work Better as One?

Exploring whether consolidation of Collingswood, Oaklyn and Woodlynne could lower taxes, strengthen schools and create a smaller, more efficient local government.

This is a proposal to study consolidation, not to merge the towns. A petition would begin a public process to determine whether consolidation actually makes financial, educational and operational sense before residents are asked to make a decision.

Why Study Consolidation?

Collingswood, Oaklyn and Woodlynne are three small neighboring communities with approximately 21,000 residents combined. They already share important connections, particularly through their schools, yet maintain separate municipal governments and school districts.

A formal study could determine whether combining some or all of these structures could provide the same or better services at a lower cost.

Potential Tax Savings

One municipality could potentially reduce duplicated administrative expenses involving finance, tax administration, public works management, legal and professional services, planning, purchasing, IT, insurance, facilities and other governmental functions.

Stronger Schools

A unified school system could potentially reduce administrative duplication while coordinating curriculum, student services, special education, transportation, facilities and long-term planning.

Smaller Government

A study could determine whether approximately 21,000 residents need three separate governmental structures or whether one government could deliver services more efficiently.

Our Schools Are Already Connected

Consolidation would not be starting from scratch. The communities already have significant educational relationships.
